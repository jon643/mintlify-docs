Once a breakout heals, it often leaves behind a reminder: a flat, discolored mark ranging from pink to red to deep brown, depending on your skin tone. This is called **post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH)** — a temporary increase in melanin production triggered by inflammation in the skin. PIH is not scarring (scarring is structural, involving a change in skin texture), but it can linger for months without the right treatment approach. The good news is that consistent exfoliation, combined with strict sun protection, is one of the most effective evidence-based strategies for fading these marks.

## How Neutralyze Targets Dark Spots

<Accordion title="Mandelic acid — gentle brightening for all skin tones">
  Mandelic acid is widely regarded as one of the most pigmentation-friendly exfoliating acids, particularly for medium to deeper skin tones where PIH tends to be more pronounced and longer-lasting. It accelerates the shedding of pigmented surface cells while stimulating the turnover of newer, more evenly pigmented cells beneath. Its larger molecular structure penetrates gradually, reducing the risk of post-procedure darkening that can paradoxically occur with more aggressive exfoliants.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Salicylic acid — prevention while you fade">
  Salicylic acid keeps pores clear so new breakouts are prevented. This is critical: every new breakout is a potential new dark mark. By stopping new blemishes from forming, salicylic acid ensures you are making forward progress rather than trading one set of marks for another.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Leave-on formats do the heavy lifting">
  All three Neutralyze products contribute to fading, but the leave-on formats — Exfoliating Pads and Renewal Complex — do the most work. Leave-on actives maintain contact with the skin continuously, whereas a rinse-off wash has only brief contact time. If you are focused on fading dark spots, prioritize consistent use of both leave-on products.
</Accordion>

## Timeline Expectations

Fading dark spots takes longer than clearing active breakouts. Here is a realistic timeline:

| Phase | What to Expect |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–4 | Active breakouts begin to reduce; new marks may slow in formation |
| Weeks 4–8 | Existing marks begin to lighten at the edges; newer marks fade faster |
| Weeks 8–16 | Significant fading for most marks; older or deeper pigmentation takes longer |
| 4–6 months | Full results for stubborn PIH, especially on deeper skin tones |

Be patient — and be consistent. Skipping days sets the clock back. The timeline above assumes daily use and, critically, daily SPF.

## The Role of SPF in Fading Dark Spots

<Warning>
  Daily SPF 30+ is non-negotiable when treating dark spots. UV radiation directly stimulates melanocytes (the cells that produce pigmentation), and even brief, incidental sun exposure — a walk to your car, sitting near a window — can darken existing marks faster than any product can fade them. Neutralyze's salicylic and mandelic acid also increase your skin's UV sensitivity, making sun protection doubly important. Apply a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher every morning as the final step of your daytime routine, and reapply if you spend extended time outdoors.
</Warning>

## Combining with Brightening Actives

Neutralyze pairs well with several popular brightening ingredients, and layering thoughtfully can accelerate your results.

<Accordion title="Niacinamide (vitamin B3)">
  Niacinamide is one of the best complements to Neutralyze for dark spot fading. It works by inhibiting the transfer of pigment from melanocytes to surface skin cells, addressing hyperpigmentation through a different mechanism than exfoliation. It is well tolerated by sensitive skin and can be layered after the Neutralyze routine has dried, morning or evening.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid)">
  Vitamin C is a potent antioxidant and pigmentation inhibitor, but strong L-ascorbic acid serums are best used at a different time of day than your Neutralyze routine to avoid pH conflicts. If you use Neutralyze in the evening, apply vitamin C in the morning before SPF — or vice versa. This separation ensures each active performs at its intended pH.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Retinoids (retinol, adapalene, tretinoin)">
  Retinoids accelerate cell turnover and are a well-established treatment for both acne and PIH. Use your retinoid at the opposite time of day from the Neutralyze leave-on products to avoid stacking too many exfoliating actives at once. Consult a dermatologist if combining prescription tretinoin with Neutralyze.
</Accordion>

<Info>
  Deep or pitted acne scarring is structural — it involves changes in the collagen architecture of the skin, not just surface pigmentation. Exfoliating acids can improve the appearance of superficial scarring over time, but significant pitting or icepick scars are best addressed by a dermatologist through in-office procedures. If you are unsure whether your marks are PIH or scarring, a dermatologist can help distinguish between the two.
</Info>
