Starting a new exfoliating acne treatment and then seeing more breakouts in the first week or two is one of the most common reasons people give up on a routine that would have worked. It can feel like the product is making things worse when, in many cases, it is actually doing exactly what it is supposed to do. This page explains the difference between **skin purging** — a normal, temporary phase — and a **product reaction** that warrants stopping use, so you can make a confident, informed decision about your skin.

## What Is Skin Purging?

Purging happens when an exfoliating active — like salicylic acid, mandelic acid, or a retinoid — accelerates your skin's natural cell turnover rate. This faster shedding brings congestion that was already forming beneath the surface (microcomedones, early clogged pores) up and out faster than it would have emerged on its own. The result looks like a sudden increase in breakouts, but the key insight is this: **those blemishes were already forming**. The active did not create them — it surfaced them sooner.

Purging is a sign that the actives are working. It is temporary, and once the backlog of subsurface congestion clears, your skin typically settles into a noticeably cleaner baseline.

**Key characteristics of purging:**
- Breakouts appear in your **usual breakout zones** — where you already tend to break out
- The uptick begins in **weeks 1–2** of starting the new routine
- Blemishes resolve and clear faster than your normal breakouts
- Overall skin begins to improve by **week 3–4** with consistent use
- There is no persistent redness, rash, or widespread irritation

## What Is a Breakout Reaction?

A product reaction is different in character, location, and trajectory. Instead of a temporary flare in familiar zones that resolves on its own, a reaction tends to spread, worsen, and involve symptoms that go beyond normal breakouts.

**Key characteristics of a reaction:**
- Breakouts appear in **areas you do not normally break out**
- The flare **does not resolve** — it continues or worsens over two or more weeks of use
- You notice **itching, burning, or stinging** that persists beyond the first application
- Skin becomes red, rashy, or shows signs of contact dermatitis (small bumps, flaking, or hives)

If your experience matches the reaction description rather than purging, reduce frequency immediately and read the next section.

## What to Do During Purging

<Steps>
  <Step title="Stay consistent — but watch closely">
    If the breakouts are in your normal zones and you are not experiencing persistent irritation, continue your routine as planned. Stopping and restarting resets the purging clock and prolongs the process. Consistent use is what allows the backlog of congestion to clear.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reduce frequency if needed">
    If purging feels intense or your skin is uncomfortable, drop the Exfoliating Pads to every other day while keeping the Face Wash and Renewal Complex daily. This slows the pace of turnover slightly without halting progress.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Support your barrier">
    During purging, your barrier can become temporarily more vulnerable. Make sure you are applying a broad-spectrum SPF 30+ every morning, staying hydrated, and avoiding other harsh or potentially irritating products (strong vitamin C serums, new actives, or physical scrubs) while you move through this phase.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give it 4 weeks before evaluating">
    The purging window is typically **2–4 weeks**. If your skin has not begun to clear by week 4–5 of consistent daily use, reassess whether you are dealing with a reaction rather than purging.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## When to Stop

Stop use and do not continue if you experience any of the following:
- Persistent burning, stinging, or pain that does not subside within a few minutes of application
- A spreading rash, hives, or signs of an allergic reaction
- Breakouts rapidly multiplying in areas entirely outside your normal breakout zones
- Swelling or warmth in the skin not associated with a blemish
