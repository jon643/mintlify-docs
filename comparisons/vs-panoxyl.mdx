PanOxyl and Neutralyze are both legitimate acne treatments, but they work through completely different mechanisms. PanOxyl's active ingredient is **benzoyl peroxide** — an antibacterial agent that kills *C. acnes* bacteria on the skin. Neutralyze uses **2% salicylic acid + 1% mandelic acid** — a dual-acid approach that unclogs pores, reduces inflammation, and fades post-acne marks. Neither is universally better; the right choice depends on your skin, your acne type, and what trade-offs matter to you.

## How Benzoyl Peroxide Works

Benzoyl peroxide releases oxygen into the follicle, creating an environment where acne-causing bacteria can't survive. It's effective at reducing bacterial load, which is one of the contributing factors to inflammatory acne. PanOxyl washes are available at 4% and 10% concentrations, with the higher concentration being notably more drying and irritating for many users.

The most well-known practical drawback of benzoyl peroxide is its effect on fabric: it **bleaches towels, pillowcases, sheets, and clothing** on contact. This isn't a formulation flaw — it's an inherent property of the ingredient itself.

## How Neutralyze's Dual-Acid Approach Works

Neutralyze takes a different route. Salicylic acid (BHA) is oil-soluble, so it travels into the pore and dissolves the oil-and-dead-skin plug that causes congestion — addressing one of the root causes of breakouts at the source. Mandelic acid (AHA) works at the surface to exfoliate dead skin cells, fade dark spots, and even out the post-acne marks that linger after breakouts heal. Because Neutralyze contains **no benzoyl peroxide**, it will not bleach your towels, pillowcases, or clothing.

## Neutralyze vs. PanOxyl: Side-by-Side

| | Neutralyze | PanOxyl (typical) |
|---|---|---|
| **Active ingredient(s)** | 2% salicylic acid + 1% mandelic acid | Benzoyl peroxide (4% or 10%) |
| **Mechanism** | Unclogs pores, exfoliates, fades marks (BHA + AHA) | Kills acne-causing bacteria (antibacterial) |
| **Bleaches fabric?** | ❌ No | ✅ Yes — a known property of benzoyl peroxide |
| **Fragrance-free** | ✅ Yes | Varies — check label |
| **Best for** | Clogged pores, blackheads, post-acne marks, daily use | Bacterial/inflammatory acne, high-bacteria environments |
| **Pore-penetrating** | ✅ Yes (salicylic acid is oil-soluble) | ❌ No (benzoyl peroxide works at the surface) |

## Which Approach Is Right for You?

If fabric bleaching is a dealbreaker, Neutralyze eliminates that concern entirely. If your acne is heavily bacterial and inflammatory, benzoyl peroxide's antibacterial action may be what your skin needs. For clogged pores, blackheads, and a formula that also works on post-acne discoloration, the dual-acid approach in Neutralyze is specifically designed for that.

<Note>
  Some people do well using both approaches — a benzoyl peroxide wash in the morning and a salicylic/mandelic acid product in the evening, for example. If you're considering combining them, consult a dermatologist to find the right routine for your skin. **Neutralyze does not contain benzoyl peroxide** and is safe to use alongside it when your doctor recommends it.
</Note>
