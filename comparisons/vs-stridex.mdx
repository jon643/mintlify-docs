Stridex is one of the most recognized names in drugstore acne pads, and for good reason — salicylic acid pads are a proven format for treating breakouts. If you're weighing Neutralyze Exfoliating Pads against Stridex (or similar drugstore pads), the core difference isn't the format: it's what's in the formula and what the formula is designed to do.

## What They Have in Common

Both Neutralyze Exfoliating Pads and Stridex use **salicylic acid** as their primary active ingredient. Both are designed for daily use on acne-prone skin, both come in convenient pre-soaked pad form, and both are widely available without a prescription.

## Where They Differ

Neutralyze Exfoliating Pads add **1% mandelic acid** alongside the salicylic acid. That second active is the meaningful formulation difference — it means Neutralyze pads address post-acne marks and surface texture in the same step as treating active breakouts, rather than leaving that job to a separate product.

Neutralyze pads are also formulated without fragrance and without parabens. Fragrance is a common irritant on acne-prone skin, and removing it reduces the risk of sensitivity reactions that can worsen redness and inflammation.

## The Practical Difference

Drugstore pads like Stridex are built to treat the active breakout. Neutralyze pads are built to treat the breakout *and* the mark it leaves behind, in a formula designed to minimize irritation for acne-prone skin. If you've ever cleared a breakout only to be left with a dark spot for months afterward, that's the gap Neutralyze is formulated to close.
